The 4 a.m. problem

A forecast in one tab. A DOT map in another. A roster that doesn’t match the building. And a decision due before sunrise.

01

Scattered inputs

Facility managers piece the call together from forecasts, road maps and school announcements, under time pressure, often alone.

02

Wrong recipients, late

Who is assigned here? Who is on detail elsewhere this week? Who is already teleworking? The message lands after people are on the road.

03

No record of why

Two managers facing identical conditions decide differently, and when a closure that should have happened didn’t, nobody can reconstruct what was known.

How it works

From raw signal to the right inbox, with a human in the middle.

  1. Ingest

    Live signals

    NWS alerts, forecasts and observations for each facility’s point and zone, plus supplementary signals: state DOT road conditions, school district closures, air quality.

    • NWS / NOAA
    • State DOT 511
    • Schools
    • AirNow
  2. Evaluate

    Facility-aware scoring

    Each input is checked against documented thresholds for that building, its shift start, its commute corridors and its people.

    • Location
    • Shift times
    • Accessibility
  3. Recommend

    Graded posture

    A recommended operating status, the inputs behind it, which threshold was crossed and by how much, a confidence level and what would change it.

    • Reasoning
    • Confidence
  4. Decide & notify

    The manager approves

    The manager accepts, adjusts or overrides. Fieldcast builds the recipient list, drafts the message, routes channels and writes it all to the decision log.

    • SMS
    • Email
    • Audit trail

Graded, not binary

Five operating postures. The same ones the federal workforce already uses.

Recommendations map to OPM-style operating status categories, so the output speaks the language employees and supervisors already know.

Threshold defensibility

Every cutoff has a source. No magic numbers.

Our thresholds are anchored to standards that already exist, so a recommendation can be defended to leadership, to employees, and after the fact.

SignalAnchorWhere it comes from
Alert seriousness Severity, certainty and urgency fields on each alert Common Alerting Protocol fields set by the NWS on every alert
Winter weather Ice Storm / Winter Storm Warning in the facility’s zone NWS warning criteria, set by each local forecast office
Extreme heat Extreme Heat Warning or high HeatRisk NWS heat products and the NWS HeatRisk scale
Road conditions Condition category on commute corridors The state DOT’s own 511 condition categories
Air quality AQI category “Unhealthy” or worse EPA Air Quality Index breakpoints via AirNow
Output posture Five graded operating statuses OPM operating status categories for federal employees

Our principle

We don’t automate the decision.
We make sure it’s a good one.

The person making the call gets everything in one place, on time. Whatever they decide reaches the right people, fast. And the record holds up afterward.
